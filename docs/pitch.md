# Pitch and demo script

One story, Alexia, told in under three minutes, hitting UBS (main), AXA and IMC. Booth variants and a Q&A cheat sheet follow.

Every number below comes from engine `core-1.1` with Alexia's demo profile and default assumptions. They were checked on Sat 21 Nov with `core.expand`, `core.explore` and `core.validation`. **If the engine or tree changes before the demo, re-check them** with the commands at the end of this file.

Lumi's lines are generated by `web/src/scene/narration.js` from engine fields, and the quotes below are what that template produces for these nodes. They are not an LLM's output.

## Booth variants (30–40 s, about 90–100 words each)

### UBS booth

> UBS told us at your booth: Monte Carlo predicts, and you want to explore. So our engine is deterministic. Every branch is a fully specified if-then world, and it reproduces your starter kit's three scenarios to the franc. On each node it asks what would have to be true: break-even salary, goal-seek, the smallest shock that breaks the plan, the cost of waiting a year, and which assumption matters most. Plan check comes first: a funding gap is never hidden behind a score. Six checks run live, and every number has a trace id. Monte Carlo survives only as an optional lens.

**Show:** the master's node → Report → *What would have to be true* → the validation tab, 6/6.

### AXA booth

> Most teams would send every payslip photo to a frontier model. We don't. The PDF's own text or local OCR goes to a small model with an 800-token budget. Then deterministic checks run: every number must appear in the document, and gross minus deductions must equal net. Only when a check fails does that one document go to a cheap vision model. On 60 synthetic Swiss documents in four languages: 97.7% field accuracy against 99.4% for the frontier model, at 154 times lower cost, and 73% of images never leave the laptop. Voice is local too: Kokoro TTS, cost zero.

(100 words, about 40 s)

## Q&A cheat sheet

### UBS scorecard questions

**Which assumptions matter most?**
The engine ranks them per node: a deterministic tornado over each assumption's sourced range, shown under *What would have to be true*. For the master's: net salary after the master's (CHF 4,200 → 5,600 moves 10-year net worth by CHF 119,835), studio rent (CHF 61,366), real wage growth (CHF 54,966), then the income-tax level and inflation (about CHF 25,000 each). Any of them can be edited in the report, and the visible tree recomputes.

**How does a decision change the state?**
A decision is a list of interventions on the monthly plan: set, add or multiply income, rent or costs over a window; one-off costs and incomes; a new loan; a change to an existing loan; or a change to an assumption on that branch only. A child applies on top of its ancestors. The engine re-runs 120 months, and every node shows *what this choice changes* and its delta against the parent. Example: the study loan adds CHF 15,000 of cash in month 0 and CHF 300 a month of repayments from month 25. That moves the funding gap from CHF 18,835 in month 7 to CHF 3,820 in month 20, and real 10-year net worth falls by CHF 1,163.

**How did you verify it?**
(1) It reproduces the UBS starter kit's three scenarios exactly (closing cash 152,000 / 144,600 / 97,500; lowest cash 9,200 / −18,600 / 2,300). (2) Six live checks, 6/6 (`GET /validation`): 8 accounting identities on all 40 nodes with a largest error of CHF 0.00; same seed gives the same trace id; higher tuition never raises cash in any month; branches are isolated; the UBS kit matches; and search thresholds are re-simulated (break-even CHF 4,844.64 holds, CHF 1 lower does not). (3) The `core/tests` and `api/tests` pytest suites. (4) Lumi's answers: every number is checked against engine tool outputs, with a template fallback.

**What happens when a constraint is violated?**
Feasibility comes first. Any funding gap or broken hard limit makes the plan check "Needs financing" or "Does not hold", whatever the secondary score. Negative cash is an unfunded need (the UBS kit's convention), never an automatic loan. Each constraint (no funding gap, debt payments ≤ 33% of income, a 3-month liquidity buffer, each goal) shows its margin, first breach month and months in breach. Lumi says so out loud. The levers and children show what would fix it: a loan narrows the gap, and the part-time master's removes it.

### Other likely questions

- **"Isn't this just a spreadsheet?"** A spreadsheet gives one path. Here, 40 decision nodes recompute lazily, with stress tests, break-evens and reverse stress on every node, every assumption is sourced and editable, and a guide explains each number without inventing any.
- **"Why not let the LLM answer?"** It can't be audited. The LLM only narrates engine output, and an answer with an unknown number is replaced by a template.
- **"Why is Monte Carlo off?"** It reads as a forecast. We keep it as an optional *uncertainty lens*, labelled "shares of simulated paths under these assumptions, not real-world probabilities".
- **"Why not use the frontier model for documents?"** It costs 150× more for 1.7 points of accuracy. We lose only on tiny, blurred bank statements, and there we ask the user for a better photo.
- **"Is Lumi giving advice?"** No. It never recommends, the agent drops sentences that recommend, and Lumi never points at a branch as "the best".
- **"Is the data real?"** No. Alexia and the profiles come from the UBS kit (synthetic). The 60 documents are rendered by our generator with invented names and IBANs.
- **"What did you build this weekend?"** Everything in `core/`, `api/`, `multimodal/` and `web/`. Existing pieces are open-source libraries, the UBS kit (as a reference to reproduce) and a message-board kit for our agents. See the README, *What we built at LauzHack*.
